# The barter economy's data desert: what we know and what's missing

**The most important finding for an industry report on barter is that the industry itself is remarkably poorly measured.** Across all six data categories requested — IRS filings, BLS labor data, IRTA trade statistics, academic research, gig platform surveys, and specific barter platform metrics — verifiable, year-by-year data for 2020–2025 is scarce. The widely cited **$12–14 billion global estimate** from IRTA has no disclosed methodology and has remained essentially unchanged for a decade, while the **"400,000+ participating businesses"** figure has circulated since at least 2008. What follows is a comprehensive accounting of every data point found, every gap identified, and every recycled statistic flagged.

---

## IRS does not publish barter-specific filing data

The IRS requires barter exchanges to report transactions on **Form 1099-B** (Box 13, "Bartering"), but it publishes no breakdowns separating barter exchange filings from securities brokerage filings. After exhaustive review of IRS Statistics of Income publications, Data Book tables (FY2020–FY2024), and GAO reports, **no publicly available IRS data provides barter-specific 1099-B counts or aggregate barter transaction volumes for any year.** Barter income is reported by taxpayers across Schedules C, 1, and E, where it is subsumed into broader income categories and never separately tabulated in SOI publications.

What the IRS *does* publish is total information returns received across all types:

| Fiscal Year | Total Information Returns (all types) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FY 2018 | ~3.5 billion | GAO-21-102 (Dec 2020) |
| FY 2022 | >5.4 billion | GAO-24-107028 (2024) |
| FY 2024 | ~4.6 billion | IRS Data Book FY2024 (May 2025) |

These aggregate figures include 1099-B among dozens of other information return types, with no sub-type breakout. The IRS Data Book Table 24 XLSX files (available at irs.gov/statistics) may contain additional granularity, but publicly visible summaries do not separate barter from securities.

**Key regulatory developments (2020–2025):** Treasury Decision 9972 (Feb 2023) lowered the e-file threshold from 250 to 10 information returns, effective January 2024 — meaning more small barter exchanges must now e-file. The new Form 1099-DA for digital assets (2025–2026) will siphon some crypto-related transactions off 1099-B but does not directly affect traditional barter exchange reporting. Three GAO reports (GAO-21-102, GAO-24-107028, GAO-24-107095) mention barter exchanges in the context of information return processing but provide no barter-specific statistics.

**Data quality note:** The only route to obtaining barter-specific 1099-B counts would be an IRS FOIA request for internal tabulations of Box 13 filings. This data almost certainly exists within the IRS but is not part of any standard public release. This is a **primary data source** assessment — directly confirmed via IRS publications at irs.gov/statistics.

## BLS tracks freelancers but not barter or alternative payment

The Bureau of Labor Statistics has **never collected data** on barter, non-monetary exchange, or alternative payment methods in labor markets. BLS compensation programs (National Compensation Survey, Employment Cost Index) track wages and employer-provided benefits only. However, BLS does provide the most authoritative data on the independent workforce that forms the labor pool for potential barter activity.

**Contingent Worker Supplement (CWS), July 2023** — published November 8, 2024 (primary data from BLS):

| Metric | May 2017 | July 2023 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contingent workers (% of employed) | 3.8% | **4.3%** | +0.5 pp |
| Independent contractors (% of employed) | 6.9% | **7.4% (11.9 million)** | +0.5 pp |
| On-call workers (% of employed) | 1.7% | 1.7% | unchanged |
| Temp agency workers (% of employed) | 0.9% | 0.6% | -0.3 pp |

The July 2023 CWS also included questions on digital platform employment, but **BLS has not yet released those results** — the agency states the release date is "to be determined." A May 2025 CWS was fielded but results remain unpublished as of March 2026, partly due to a federal government shutdown in October–November 2025 disrupting BLS schedules.

**No CWS was conducted in 2020, 2021, 2022, or 2024**, creating a significant gap. The survey has only been fielded in 2005, 2017, 2023, and 2025 during the past two decades. BLS explicitly states it "does not have a definition of the gig economy or gig workers."

**Non-BLS government sources** fill some gaps. The Federal Reserve's 2024 SHED survey found **20% of adults** performed gig activities in the prior month, with **9%** doing short-task work and **4%** using platforms like rideshare/delivery apps. Census Bureau data shows **30.4 million nonemployer businesses** operated in 2023, while IRS records show **31 million Schedule C filings** with $410.7 billion in combined net nonfarm income for Tax Year 2022.

## IRTA's industry estimates lack methodology and annual granularity

The International Reciprocal Trade Association is the barter industry's primary trade body, yet it publishes **no annual reports, no audited statistics, and no year-by-year transaction data.** The organization's publicly available data points are standing estimates that have evolved slowly over more than a decade:

| Claim | Current Version | Earlier Versions | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global barter volume | **$12–14 billion/year** | $10B (~2008); $9.1B (late 1990s) | ⚠️ No methodology disclosed; IRTA acknowledges "could vary widely" |
| Participating businesses | **400,000+ worldwide** | 450,000+ (2008); 470,000 (various) | ⚠️ Static for 15+ years across sources |
| Sector split | Countertrade 50%, Corporate 30%, Retail 20% | — | Self-reported; unverifiable |
| IRTA members | **80+ member exchanges** in 15+ countries | — | Current, from irta.com |

The **Universal Currency (UC) inter-exchange platform** provides some of the only concrete transaction data: **$14.5 million** in trading in 2019 (an all-time record) and over **$1 million/month** in early 2022 (~$3.26 million in Q1 2022). These are primary figures from IRTA press releases but represent only inter-exchange clearing, not total industry volume.

**NATE (National Association of Trade Exchanges)** claims **50+ member barter companies** representing **20,000+ businesses** but publishes no trade volume statistics. NATE files IRS Form 990-N (for very small nonprofits), indicating minimal organizational revenue.

The often-cited claim that **"65% of Fortune 500 companies use barter"** traces to Active International marketing materials and cannot be independently verified. Similarly, the claim that **"20–25% of world trade involves barter/countertrade"** is attributed to a U.S. Department of Commerce estimate of uncertain vintage — the original source could not be located.

**⚠️ Flag for report:** Nearly every barter industry statistic in circulation ultimately traces back to IRTA's self-reported estimates or individual company marketing claims. No independent third-party audit or verification of these figures exists.

## Academic evidence supports counter-cyclical barter but nothing covers 2021–2024

The academic literature offers strong foundational evidence that barter and complementary currencies are counter-cyclical — rising during recessions and periods of monetary stress — but **no peer-reviewed study specifically examines whether U.S. barter activity increased during the 2021–2024 inflation period.**

**Core empirical studies on barter's counter-cyclical nature:**

The most rigorous work comes from **Marvasti and Smyth (1998, 2001, 2011)**, who used IRTA data on organized barter exchanges in econometric models. Their findings: **barter transactions are counter-cyclical regardless of firm size, and barter rises with inflationary trends, technology adoption, and tax rates.** Their 2011 paper in *The American Economist* used Box-Jenkins ARMA and Kalman filter methods to address missing data in IRTA records. However, their dataset does not extend beyond ~2010.

**Stodder (2009)** and **Stodder and Lietaer (2016)** studied Switzerland's WIR system (50,000+ member businesses, ~CHF 1.5 billion annual turnover) and found **strongly counter-cyclical behavior**: WIR credit usage rises in recessions and its macroeconomic stabilizing effect exceeds its direct turnover. These are published in *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization* and *Comparative Economic Studies*, respectively.

**COVID-19 period evidence** comes primarily from developing-country community currencies. **Mattsson et al. (2023)** in *Nature Scientific Reports* documented that Kenya's Sarafu blockchain currency grew from **8,354 accounts in January 2020 to nearly 55,000 by June 2021**, with transaction volumes **ten times higher** in April 2020 than February 2020. **Mqamelo (2022)** in *Frontiers in Blockchain* published the first randomized control trial on community currencies, finding positive effects on local economic engagement during COVID in Nairobi.

The most important recent contribution is **Wong (2026)**, a working paper studying Toronto's Bunz barter community (~10,000 daily active users). This provides **the first transaction-level microdata on barter and money in a real-world field setting** — a 5x increase in digital token supply raised total trades by ~57%. Wong explicitly notes: "Since it is difficult to obtain data on barter in the field, existing studies generally only examine data on money-mediated transactions."

**Time banking research** has grown but focuses on health and social outcomes rather than economic scale. **Lu et al. (2024, 2025)** published multiple quasi-experimental studies in *Innovation in Aging* and *The Gerontologist* showing time banking promotes volunteering among older adults.

**Key gap:** No updated Marvasti-Smyth-style analysis has been published for the post-2020 period, and no academic paper directly tests the "barter as inflation hedge" hypothesis using 2021–2024 data. A 2023 SSRN working paper by **Taskinsoy** discusses barter's "reincarnation" but is a narrative review, not an empirical study.

## Gig platform surveys ignore barter; freelance workforce reaches 73 million

Major gig economy surveys provide robust workforce sizing but **none address barter interest, alternative payment preferences, or non-monetary exchange.** Every major survey — Upwork, Fiverr, MBO Partners, Payoneer — focuses on workforce size, earnings, AI adoption, and satisfaction.

**Key workforce estimates (primary data from surveys):**

| Source | Year | Independent Workers | Key Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upwork "Freelance Forward" | 2023 | **64 million** (38% of U.S. workforce) | $1.27 trillion contributed to economy |
| Upwork "Future Workforce Index" | 2025 | **~20 million** knowledge workers freelancing (28%) | $1.5 trillion collective earnings (2024) |
| MBO Partners "State of Independence" | 2024 | **72.7 million** total independents | 27.7M full-time; 4.7M earning $100K+ |
| MBO Partners "State of Independence" | 2025 | **72.9 million** total independents | Record 5.6M earning $100K+ (nearly double 2020) |
| Fiverr Impact Report | 2024 | — | 71% of freelancers focused exclusively on freelancing (up from 61% in 2021) |

**Note on Upwork's figures:** The 2023 "64 million" and 2025 "20 million" figures are not contradictory — the 2025 report narrowed scope to knowledge workers only, while the 2023 figure used a broader definition encompassing all freelance activity.

**Crypto payment adoption** is the closest proxy for alternative payment interest. A **BVNK/YouGov survey** (Sept–Oct 2025, 4,658 adults in 15 countries) found that freelancers and gig workers receiving stablecoin payments get ~35% of income this way. The **Request Finance** platform processed $30.6 million in crypto payments in November 2025 alone, with over $1.2 billion cumulative. However, the commonly cited **Humans.net survey** claiming "38% of freelancers use crypto" dates from ~2018 and should be treated as outdated. A "Triple-A" study claiming 61% of freelancers own crypto and "14 million crypto-freelancers" by end of 2023 could not be independently verified.

**The Freelancers Union** has not published an independent workforce survey since 2019, when the joint "Freelancing in America" series with Upwork ended.

## Barter platform data is almost entirely unavailable for 2020–2025

The specific barter platforms examined are overwhelmingly private companies that do not publish operational metrics. This section represents the most complete public data compilation available.

**Platforms with some verifiable data:**

| Platform | Members/Users | Transaction Volume | Period | Source Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BizX** | 7,000+ businesses | ~$13M BizX dollars (2020); +265 new members during COVID | 2020–2021 | Primary (PR Newswire press releases) |
| **ITEX** (OTCQB: ITEX) | Not disclosed recently | $5.59M revenue (trailing 12 months, Apr 2025); down from $15.8M peak (FY2012) | 2025 | Primary (financial data from public filings) |
| **hOurworld** | **29,016 members**, 358 time banks | **3,649,332 hours exchanged** (cumulative) | Cumulative as of 2026 | Primary (hourworld.org live counter) |
| **BarterPay/Tradebank Canada** | ~4,000 businesses, 18 cities | ~$40M in barter transactions (2019) | 2019 | Primary (CBC News interview with founders) |
| **IMS Barter** | 16,000+ businesses, 23,000 cardholders, 52 U.S. markets | $12.2M monthly (Dec 2007) | Claims undated; last verifiable data 2007 | Mixed — website claims (possibly stale) |

**Platforms with minimal or no public data:**

**Simbi (simbi.com)** raised $1.2 million in seed funding from Greylock Discovery Fund and Y Combinator in 2016, launching with 15,000 services on the platform. **No public data exists for 2017–2025.** The website remains active but appears to operate at very small scale with no published user metrics.

**Bartercard** was privatized in 2018 when founders Tony Wiese and Raj Pathak bought it back from ASX-listed IncentiaPay. At peak, Bartercard claimed ~55,000 global cardholders across 8+ countries. **No public financial filings exist for 2020–2025.** The related **Qoin cryptocurrency token**, launched in 2020 by Bartercard's founders, claimed $2.5 billion in transactions across 554,000 transactions (Jan 2020–Dec 2023) and 30,000+ registered businesses — but **ASIC filed a civil lawsuit** against BPS Financial in October 2022 for misleading/deceptive representations, and a class action seeking $100M was also filed. Qoin data should be treated with extreme caution.

**ITEX Corporation** offers the only long-run public data through SEC filings, but the trajectory is one of decline: from **23,500 members and $140M GMV in FY2012** to trailing 12-month revenue of just $5.59M in 2025, with a market capitalization of ~$7.24 million. ITEX has a troubled regulatory history including SEC fraud charges against former executives.

**Tradebank** and other smaller exchanges (TradeFirst, Barter Business Unlimited, etc.) publish no operational data. The industry consists primarily of regional operators serving hundreds to low thousands of members each.

## What the bartering platform market reports actually measure

Several market research firms have published reports on the "bartering platform market," but these measure something fundamentally different from total barter transaction volume:

| Report Publisher | Market Defined | Size (2024) | Projected (2030) | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 360iResearch / Research and Markets | Bartering Platform Market | $1.04 billion | $1.49 billion | 6.10% |
| Research and Markets (Sept 2024) | Virtual Barter Software Market | $793.53 million | $1.09 billion | 5.29% |
| MarketReportAnalytics | Goods Bartering Platform Market | ~$2 billion | — | 15% |

**These figures represent the revenue of barter technology/platform companies — not the total value of goods and services exchanged through barter.** They should not be confused with IRTA's $12–14 billion estimate, which attempts to capture total transaction volume across all organized barter (retail exchanges, corporate barter, and countertrade).

## Conclusion

This research reveals a fundamental measurement problem at the heart of the barter economy. **The industry's most-cited statistics are self-reported estimates from a trade association that has not disclosed its methodology and has kept its headline figures virtually unchanged for over a decade.** The IRS collects barter transaction data through 1099-B filings but does not publish barter-specific breakdowns. BLS does not track non-monetary exchange at all. Academic researchers have repeatedly noted the difficulty of obtaining field data on barter.

Three findings stand out for an industry report. First, the organized barter sector appears to be **stagnant or contracting** based on the only verifiable time-series data available: ITEX's revenue declined from $15.8 million to $5.6 million over a decade, and no platform has published growth metrics showing expansion during the 2021–2024 inflation period. Second, the **broader independent workforce is growing** — from 38.2 million in 2020 to 72.9 million in 2025 by MBO Partners' count — but no survey evidence connects this growth to increased barter interest. Third, the **academic counter-cyclical hypothesis remains untested** for the most recent inflation cycle; the foundational Marvasti-Smyth work using IRTA data has not been updated since 2011.

Any industry report should be transparent about these data limitations rather than recycling unverified estimates. The most honest framing: **organized barter is a real but small sector — likely low single-digit billions in the U.S. — operating in a near-total data vacuum, with no reliable mechanism for tracking its size or growth trajectory.**
