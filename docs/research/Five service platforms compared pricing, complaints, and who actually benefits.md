# Five service platforms compared: pricing, complaints, and who actually benefits

**The marketplace for exchanging services is fractured across radically different models — from Fiverr's 20% commission to Simbi's completely cashless credit system — and none serve all professionals well.** Each platform optimizes for a different user and extracts value in a different way. Thumbtack and Bark charge professionals for leads that often never convert. Fiverr takes the industry's highest cut from both sides. Bartercard drains cash through fees and taxes on non-cash income. And Simbi, the only truly free option, struggles with a user base too small to deliver consistent value. This comparison maps the real economics, real complaints, and real fit for each platform as of 2024–2025.

## Simbi: a noble experiment constrained by scale

Simbi positions itself as **"the world's largest non-profit mutual aid network"** — a 501(c)(3) where members exchange services using internal credits (₴) worth zero dollars. Founded by Kjerstin Erickson (Y Combinator S16, $1.2M seed from Greylock and Crunchfund), the platform peaked around 2017 before Erickson's departure in 2019. It transitioned to an all-volunteer operation and today runs on donations and grants alone.

The credit system gives new members **50₴ on signup** to start "purchasing" services. Credits are earned by providing services, volunteering for nonprofits on the platform, or referring new users. Services typically price at **50–100 credits per hour**. No credits can be purchased with real money or cashed out — this is the system's philosophical core and its practical limitation. A "Newbi" onboarding requirement forces new users to first trade with other newcomers, further restricting the already thin pool of active participants.

**The platform's central problem is density.** Simbi's own 2022 community letter conceded that *"by far the largest complaint we receive in anonymous feedback surveys is that these days it's pretty rare to find many other Simbi members in your local area."* This creates a catch-22: existing users won't recommend it because there aren't enough members, and growth stalls without recommendations.

Dispute resolution is essentially nonexistent. The terms state: *"All disputes regarding transactions are between the parties to the transaction; Simbi Community Development is not involved in any way."* No escrow, no mediation, no buyer protection. Trust relies entirely on social verification and reviews.

**Representative user complaints:**

- *"I've been surprised by the very limited services I can find on here, and how difficult it's been to find anyone interested in 'trading.' I'm simply not interested in trading my hard-earned skills for a 20 minute ukelele lesson or tarot card reading."* — App Store
- *"Due to the poor quality of work and terrible experiences I've had trying to trade graphic design work for other services, I've since stopped using it. Investing money holds people accountable."* — App Store
- *"❤️ the idea behind Simbi, and hate this terrible app. It's IMPOSSIBLE to upload, take, or attach images. I get notified of messages 8-24 hours late."* — Google Play

**Best for:** Cash-poor, time-rich individuals seeking community-oriented skill exchange — hobbyists, students, retirees, and new freelancers building portfolios. **Worst for:** Anyone needing reliable income, local in-person services, quality guarantees, or a polished digital experience.

## Thumbtack charges professionals for leads that mostly don't convert

Thumbtack connects customers with local service professionals across **1,100+ categories** in all 50 U.S. states, handling roughly **160,000 customer requests per week**. The platform is free for customers and monetizes entirely through charges to professionals — a pay-per-lead model that has grown dramatically more expensive since 2022.

### The lead pricing math

Professionals pay per lead regardless of outcome. Thumbtack's "Instant Match" system (launched 2017) automatically generates quotes when customers submit requests, charging pros when matched — even if the customer never responds. Lead costs are dynamically priced weekly based on job size, service type, geography, and competition. The same lead is sent to **multiple professionals simultaneously** (up to 10+), each charged independently.

|-----------------|------------------------|
| Tutoring / Lessons | $10–$30 |
| Appliance Repair | $18–$30+ |
| House Cleaning | $15–$50 |
| Plumbing | ~$20 avg |
| Handyman | $20–$60+ |
| Painting | $30–$60+ |
| Photography | $20–$75 |
| Home Organization | $36–$80+ |
| Event Planning / DJs | $30–$100 |
| Major Remodeling | $50–$170 |

The effective customer acquisition cost tells the real story. With reported conversion rates of **10–30%** and average lead costs of $20–$50, professionals often spend **$67–$250 to acquire a single paying customer**. One handyman reported: *"Out of 8 leads (for which I paid $140) I managed to agree on work with only one. The cost of the work was $120."*

## Fiverr extracts the highest fees among major freelance platforms

Fiverr (NYSE: FVRR) connects buyers with freelancers across **700+ categories**, processing **$391.5M in revenue** in FY2024. The platform has been steadily losing active buyers — down from ~4.2M in mid-2023 to **3.3M by Q3 2025** — while increasing spend per buyer from $265 to **$330**, reflecting a deliberate shift upmarket.

### Fee breakdown

**Seller fees:**
- **20% flat commission** on all earnings — gig price, extras, add-ons, and tips
- No reduction for volume, tenure, or seller level
- Optional Seller Plus subscriptions: $15–$49/month for analytics and faster payouts
- Withdrawal fees: $1–$3 depending on method
- Promoted Gigs: additional variable cost for search visibility
- Company-wide effective take rate: **27.6%** (per FY2024 filings); one community analysis put it at **31.8%** including Promoted Gigs and Seller Plus

**Buyer fees:**
- **5.5% service fee** on every transaction (gig price, extras, and tips)
- **$2 surcharge** on orders under $50
- A $20 gig actually costs **$23.10** after fees; a $500 gig costs **$527.50**

| Fee Component | Fiverr | Upwork | Freelancer.com |
|--------------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Seller commission | **20%** | 10% | 10% or $5 min |
| Buyer fee | **5.5% + $2 small order** | 5% | 3% or $3 min |
| Payment timing | 7–14 days | Weekly | Milestone-based |

### Top 5 complaints (2024–2025)

**1. Account suspensions without warning.** The most volcanic complaint across all platforms. Sellers report years of work vaporized overnight with no explanation and no appeal. *"After 10 years of loyal work on Fiverr, my account was suddenly disabled without warning or explanation."* — Trustpilot, March 2026. *"I was trying to offer article creation as a service... In my description, I put 'Will include reviews'. They took this as that I was selling reviews... 'We recognize this was not your intent, however, we are unable to reverse this decision.'"* — Reddit, January 2024.

**2. Scripted, unhelpful customer support.** *"Most of their customer support team members sent copy-paste messages. They didn't check any seller's messages properly."* — Trustpilot. *"For days I have not received help, each time I try, I get the same copy paste that does not apply to my issue AT ALL, and then they just close the ticket."* — Trustpilot.

**3. Fee extraction at every stage.** *"According to the new annual report, Fiverr increased take rate to 31.8%, including freelancer's costs of promoted gigs and seller plus subscriptions. I bet shareholders like that but as a user, both buyer and seller, I'm telling you, this is crazy!"* — Fiverr Community Forum.

**4. Quality issues and fake reviews.** *"All 4 freelancers were scams despite having multiple 5-star reviews. Apparently you can use fake reviews on Fiverr and also accumulate positive reviews for tasks and then create a new service for a more complex skill but the reviews still carry over."* — Upskillwise commenter, May 2025.

**5. Algorithm dependency and traffic decline.** *"Fiverr used to be about 50% of my income. Today, it's like 15% and shrinking. They've instituted a number of inane policies that are punishing and difficult to recover from."* — Reddit, July 2023.

### Who Fiverr fails

Fiverr is **least suited for freelance writers** (chronically undervalued, AI suspicion leads to cancellations), **audio professionals/musicians** (craftsmanship commoditized alongside novices), **high-end brand designers** (the $5-logo stigma persists), **complex project specialists** (the gig model breaks down for iterative, scope-changing work), and **experienced professionals seeking fair rates** (the 20% cut plus race-to-the-bottom dynamics make sustainable pricing difficult).

**Best for:** Buyers seeking quick, well-defined, low-cost tasks (image editing, data entry, short translations) and beginner freelancers building portfolios in niche categories with low competition.

## Bartercard turns spare capacity into a parallel currency — at a price

Bartercard is the world's largest commercial barter exchange, founded in 1991 in Gold Coast, Australia. With approximately **35,000 cardholders across 5–8 countries** (Australia, New Zealand, UK, Thailand, South Africa, UAE, Cyprus, and the US), it operates a fundamentally different model from the other platforms here: a multi-party trade dollar economy managed by professional trade brokers.

### How trade dollars actually work

Each member gets an electronic account. When a restaurant serves a Bartercard member dinner, it receives trade dollars (T$) credited to its account. It can then spend those trade dollars with any other member in the network — a printer, a plumber, a hotel — without the original buyer being involved. This solves barter's classic "double coincidence of wants" problem. New members receive an **interest-free line of credit** (typically T$2,500–$25,000 depending on country and tier) to start spending before earning.

Trade dollars are valued **1:1 with local currency** for tax purposes. The Australian Tax Office, HMRC, and IRS all treat T$1 as equivalent to $1 of taxable income. **Trade dollars cannot be directly converted to cash** — though members informally buy goods with trade dollars and resell them for cash. The informal discount rate is significant: independent forums have estimated the real value of a Bartercard dollar at roughly **$0.25–$0.33**.

### The fee structure creates substantial cash drain

| Fee Type | Australia | New Zealand | United States |
|----------|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Joining fee | ~A$695–$1,495 | NZ$695 | US$799 |
| Monthly fee | **A$49/mo** | **NZ$59/mo** | **US$29/mo** |
| Transaction fee (each side) | **~6.5% cash** | **6.5% cash** | **6.5% cash** |
| Trade fee | ~1% in trade dollars | 1% in trade dollars | Not confirmed |
| Round-trip cost (buy + sell) | **~13% cash + 2% trade** | ~13% cash + 2% trade | ~13% cash |

The hidden cost is taxes. On T$30,000 of income, a member owes income tax and GST/VAT in **real cash** — even though the "income" was received as non-cash trade credits. One detailed analysis estimated worst-case cash drain on T$30,000 in trade income at approximately **$15,000** when combining fees, income tax, and GST.

### What members actually say

**Positive:** *"We traded approximately 2 million annually on Bartercard... able to turn $150k of dead stock into $300k of Barter."* — NZ manufacturer. Long-tenured members in high-margin businesses with genuine spare capacity often find value.

**Negative — difficulty spending credits:** *"We have amassed a large amount of Barter dollars over the years, but we have found it almost impossible to spend these."* — Trustpilot Australia. *"I found that after a while I couldn't spend my earned 'cash'. I would ring people in the directories just to find that they too had amassed too much."* — Whirlpool Forum.

**Negative — fee structure:** *"Keep away from this Mob! They charge you dollars in fees & give you barter credit in return."* — ProductReview Australia. *"This company is bordering on fraudulent and a scam. They charge you large amounts of money every month for Marketing and Support but provide none."* — Trustpilot UK.

**Negative — price inflation:** *"We used to put an extra 25% on top of our normal prices to cover the ridiculous fees."* — Whirlpool. *"Prices on items are triple than RRP."* — ProductReview.

A former trade broker revealed a tiered system: *"I had a list of 128 clients — 2 of who were satisfied Bartercard members... Bartercard does not belong in a small or medium sized company because of their fees."*

| Feature | Bartercard (Commercial) | Informal Peer-to-Peer Barter |
|---------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Parties | Multi-party network (35,000+) | Bilateral (1-to-1) |
| Timing | Asynchronous (sell now, buy later) | Simultaneous exchange required |
| Value matching | Trade dollar currency handles imbalance | Must be roughly equal value |
| Record-keeping | Electronic centralized ledger | Manual/informal |
| Tax compliance | Fully tracked and taxable | Often overlooked |
| Credit facility | Interest-free line of credit | None |
| Intermediary | Trade brokers facilitate matches | None |
| Cash fees | ~13% round-trip | $0 |

**Best for:** High-margin service businesses with genuine spare capacity (restaurants with empty tables, hotels with vacant rooms) that can spend all trade dollars on deductible business expenses. **Worst for:** Low-margin businesses, cash-strapped operations (Bartercard drains cash despite marketing claims), and businesses in areas with few active members.

## Bark.com promises choice but delivers expensive uncertainty

Bark.com connects customers with professionals across **2,000+ service categories in 8 countries**, with its strongest presence in the UK. Unlike Thumbtack's auto-charge model, Bark lets professionals **choose** which leads to pursue — but they must spend credits to unlock contact information before making any contact, creating a different kind of gamble.

### Credit economics

Credits cost **$2.35 each** (US) or **£1.20 + VAT** (UK). Each lead requires **6–28 credits** depending on service type and estimated job value, translating to roughly **$14–$65+ per lead**. A wedding photography lead costs approximately 10 credits ($23.50); an aerial photography lead can run 28 credits ($65.80). As of **November 2025**, all newly purchased credits expire after just **3 months** — a major policy change that has generated significant backlash.

An optional **Elite Pro subscription** provides 5 free leads per month and a 20% credit discount, but requires no qualification — it is purely pay-to-play, despite being marketed as a prestige designation.

## Choosing the right platform depends on what you're actually trading

The five platforms serve fundamentally different needs, and the worst mistake a professional can make is choosing based on marketing claims rather than economic reality.

**If you need real income from client work**, Fiverr offers the largest buyer pool but takes the industry's steepest cut (20% seller + 5.5% buyer). It works for beginners building portfolios and niche specialists with low competition — but experienced professionals consistently report that the platform's race-to-the-bottom dynamics and algorithm dependence make it unsustainable as a primary income source.

**If you provide local services in the US**, Thumbtack delivers the highest lead volume but at rapidly escalating costs. The math works for high-ticket jobs ($1,000+) and recurring services. It breaks down completely for anyone charging under $150 per job, where lead costs can consume the entire project fee.

**If you're UK-based and want lead control**, Bark lets you choose which leads to pursue rather than being auto-charged — but the leads are overwhelmingly described as non-responsive, and the new 3-month credit expiry pressures professionals into wasteful spending.

**If you run a business with genuine spare capacity**, Bartercard offers the most sophisticated barter infrastructure but imposes the heaviest hidden costs. The **~13% cash transaction fees** plus full tax obligations on trade dollar income mean this only works for high-margin businesses that can direct all trade spending to deductible expenses.

**If you're cash-poor but skill-rich**, Simbi is the only genuinely free option — but its user base has contracted to the point where finding active trading partners, especially locally, is the platform's own acknowledged primary challenge. It works best as a community experience rather than a reliable service exchange.

No platform has solved the fundamental tension between generating revenue from professionals and delivering enough value to justify the cost. The professionals who succeed on any of these platforms share common traits: they treat the platform as **one channel among many**, respond to leads instantly, maintain competitive pricing, and rigorously track their return on investment rather than hoping the next lead will be different.
